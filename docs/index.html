How a page is built

Every page has two parts: a hero at the top, which is always there, and a stack of sections below it, which you control completely.

The hero holds the badge, headline, intro line, buttons and banner image. It stays fixed at the top because the badge doubles as the page's main heading for search engines.

Everything under the hero is yours to arrange. You add sections, drag them into the order you want, and hide the ones you do not need. Nothing is locked, and nothing requires a developer.

Where to find it

Open any page, service, industry or location in the WordPress admin. Below the main editor you will see two panels: Hero and Page sections.

Working with sections

  1. Add. Click Add section at the bottom of the Page sections panel and pick a layout from the list.
  2. Reorder. Drag a section by its header bar. The page re-flows automatically.
  3. Hide. Switch on Hide this section. The section disappears from the live page but keeps all its content, and its header gains a red (hidden) label so you can see at a glance what is switched off.
  4. Collapse. Click a section's header to fold it away while you work on others.
  5. Duplicate. Use the section's own menu to copy it, content and all.

Each section also carries a kicker (the small label above the heading), a title, and a lede (the intro paragraph). Leave any of them blank to omit it.

Tip

In a title, wrap one phrase in asterisks to give it the cyan-to-blue gradient: IT that keeps your business *running and secure*. Use it once per title — it stops working as emphasis if everything is highlighted.

Tighter padding and anchors

Tighter padding reduces the space above and below a section, useful when two related sections should read as one block. Anchor id gives the section a name you can link to from elsewhere on the page, for example #book-a-call.

Backgrounds

Every section has a Background setting with four choices.

SettingWhat it does
AutoThe default. Alternates white and pale tint down the page on its own, so the rhythm stays right however you reorder things.
WhiteForces plain white.
Light tintForces the pale blue-grey.
Deep navyForces the dark band. Text and cards invert automatically.
One rule the page enforces for you

Two dark bands can never sit next to each other. If you set a section to Deep navy and the one above it is already dark, the second renders as tint instead.

This is not a bug. Stacked dark bands merge into one heavy slab and the page loses its sense of structure, so the design system forbids it and the page simply will not let it happen.

The same applies to the CTA band: it paints its own gradient and counts as a dark step, so a section placed straight after one will not render dark either.

Which should I reach for?

Try a purpose-built layout first. They carry the spacing, type sizes and colour decisions already made for that kind of content, so they look finished immediately. Use rich_text or elements when your content genuinely does not match any of them.

Free elements

The Free elements section is the escape hatch. Inside it you stack individual elements in any order, across one, two or three columns.

Add a section, choose Free elements, then use its own Add element button. Drag elements to reorder them exactly as you do with sections.

ElementOptions
HeadingThree sizes, left or centred, asterisk highlighting works here too
TextFull editor, images can be dropped inline
ImageFull, half or one-third width, with an optional caption
ButtonThree styles, left or centred
Tick listCheck marks or minus signs
StatsTwo, three or four across
CardIcon, title, text, optional link, optional gradient treatment
QuoteFree text with a name and role, not tied to a saved testimonial
ShortcodeAnything, optionally in a white card
SpacerSmall, medium or large gap
DividerA hairline rule
Why this still looks right

Every element is drawn by the same code the purpose-built sections use. A card here is the same card as in a card grid; stats are the same stats. You get freedom of arrangement without the page drifting away from the brand.

Columns respond on their own: three drop to two on tablets and to one on phones.

Forms and shortcodes

A shortcode is a short piece of text in square brackets that the site swaps for something real — most often a contact form.

Getting the shortcode

  1. Go to Fluent Forms → All Forms in the admin sidebar.
  2. Find your form in the list. The Shortcode column shows something like [fluentform id="3"].
  3. Copy it.

Four places you can paste it

WhereBest for
Shortcode / embed sectionA form that is the point of the section. Choose narrow or full width, boxed or bare.
FAQ section → Right-hand panel set to FormA form beside the questions. Fill in Fluent Forms shortcode.
Rich text section → Shortcode fieldA form directly under some explaining text.
Free elements → Shortcode elementA form anywhere in a stack of other elements.
If you see placeholder text

“Add a Fluent Forms shortcode to this section” means the shortcode field is still empty. Scroll down inside that section and paste it in.

Any shortcode works in all four places, not only forms — so a map, a booking calendar or a video embed from another plugin goes in exactly the same way.

The CTA band

The gradient band that invites people to get in touch. It appears above the footer on every page except Contact, and you can also drop one mid-page.

Editing the copy

Open Hero → CTA band on any page. Change the heading, the text, the button, or any combination.

LevelApplies to
A CTA band sectionThat one band only
Hero → CTA band tabThat one page
Site SettingsEvery page that has not overridden it
Each field falls through on its own

Change only the heading on a page and its text and button still come from Site Settings. You never have to retype copy you did not want to change, and updating the site-wide text still reaches every page that left it alone.

Turning it off, or moving it

Switch off Show the CTA band above the footer to remove it from a page. To place one mid-page instead, add a CTA band section wherever you like — you can have both.

The hero

The hero panel sits above Page sections and holds the top of the page.

  • Kicker badge — the small pill. This is the page's main heading for search engines, so keep it short and descriptive.
  • Display title — the large headline. Asterisks give the gradient highlight.
  • Lede — one or two sentences of intro.
  • Buttons — a primary and an optional secondary. On phones they stretch to full width so they line up.
  • Reassurance points — up to three short micro-points with icons.
  • Banner image — with alt text for accessibility.
  • Hide image on mobile — keeps the image on desktop and drops it on phones, where it can crowd the text.
