What we believe

Problems businesses shouldn't still be having in 2026

These aren't hypotheticals. Every one of them is something we found in a client's environment during onboarding.

  • A backup job that reported success for eleven months and had never been restored.
  • Nine former employees with active accounts, two of them administrators.
  • An outage timeline that nobody could reconstruct because no one was monitoring.
  • An IT invoice that went up the month everything broke.
  • A first-line support script read by someone with no ability to fix the problem.

Our story

Built in Raleigh, deliberately small at the start

We launched in 2024 with a narrow proposition: Microsoft-certified engineers, based in the US, doing managed IT properly for businesses in the Triangle. We took on fewer clients than we could have in the first year because onboarding well takes longer than onboarding fast, and a client rushed through onboarding is a client with a bad first quarter.

That decision shaped how we work now. We still run a structured assessment before quoting, we still publish response targets we can meet, and we still tell prospects when a project or co-managed arrangement would serve them better than a full agreement.

Today we support businesses across Raleigh, Durham, Chapel Hill, Cary and Charlotte, in healthcare, legal, financial and professional services.

How we work

Assess before we quote

No proposal is written from a phone call. We look at the environment first so the number we give you is the number you pay.

Publish what we'll do, then report on it

Response targets, patch compliance and roadmap progress go in a quarterly review you can hold us to.

Say no when the fit is wrong

We refer work we're not the right partner for. A bad-fit client is worse for us than no client.

Core values

Four commitments, each with a test attached

A value you can't fail isn't a value. Each of these has a specific way we'd know we'd broken it.

Certified, US-based, named

You know which engineer owns your environment. Test: can you name them?

Answer inside the hour

One business hour to first human response. Test: it's in the quarterly report, met or missed.

Prevent, don't bill

Flat pricing so we lose when you have incidents. Test: ticket volume should fall year on year.

Say it plainly

No jargon in a client-facing document. Test: could your office manager act on it?
