The 9 controls cyber insurers now expect — and how to evidence each one

Underwriters stopped taking “we have antivirus” as an answer some time ago. Here is what is actually being asked on 2026 renewal questionnaires, and what counts as proof.

AM Written by A. Mensah, Senior Security Engineer Microsoft Certified: Security Operations Analyst · Reviewed 14 August 2026 · 9 min read
On this page

  • The nine controls
  • The three gaps we find most often
  • What to do before renewal

Why the questionnaires changed

Between 2022 and 2025, ransomware loss ratios pushed cyber insurers from broad coverage toward control-based underwriting. The practical effect for a 40-person firm in Raleigh is that the renewal form got longer, more technical, and materially harder to answer honestly without help.

Most of the businesses we onboard can answer six of the nine questions below. The three they can't are almost always the same three — and they're the ones that move the premium.

If you cannot produce the evidence within a week of being asked, underwriting treats the control as absent — regardless of whether it is technically in place.

The nine controls

Each control below appears on the majority of 2026 questionnaires we have reviewed. The evidence column is what an underwriter or broker will actually accept — a screenshot of a settings page is rarely enough on its own.

The nine controls, what is asked, and the evidence underwriters accept
ControlWhat's askedAccepted evidence
MFA on emailEnforced for 100% of users, including executivesConditional access policy export + exception report
MFA on remote accessVPN and any published applicationPolicy export, no legacy-auth exemptions
EDR coverageDeployed on every endpoint and serverAgent-coverage report vs. asset inventory
Immutable backupsOffline or immutable copy, tested restoresRestore test report with date and RTO achieved
Privileged access separationAdmin accounts distinct from daily accountsDirectory export showing role assignments
Patch cadenceCritical patches inside 14 daysPatch-compliance report over 90 days
Email filteringAdvanced phishing and impersonation protectionPolicy configuration + quarantine statistics
Security awareness trainingAnnual minimum, phishing simulationCompletion rates and simulation click-through
Incident response planDocumented, with named rolesThe plan itself, plus evidence of a tabletop exercise

The three gaps we find most often

Privileged access separation, tested restores and a documented incident response plan. All three are inexpensive to fix and all three are disproportionately weighted by underwriters, because each one materially changes loss outcomes rather than just reducing incident frequency.

1 · Admin accounts doing daily work

A single account used for both email and tenant administration turns one successful phish into a full compromise. Separating them takes an afternoon and needs no new licensing.

2 · Backups that have never been restored

A backup job reporting success is not a recovery capability. Until a restore has been performed and timed, the recovery time objective on the questionnaire is a guess.

3 · An incident response plan nobody has read

Underwriters increasingly ask for evidence of a tabletop exercise, not just the document. A two-hour walkthrough with named roles and a written outcome satisfies the question and usually surfaces two or three gaps of its own.

What to do before renewal

Start ninety days out. Pull the evidence for the six controls you already hold so the pack is ready, then spend the remaining time closing the three that are missing — in that order, because the evidence-gathering is what runs long, not the remediation.

If your broker has already sent the questionnaire, work backwards from its wording rather than from a generic framework. Underwriters accept the evidence their own form describes, and the phrasing differs enough between carriers to matter.
